# Limited base-pretraining tranche: plan, registered criteria and stop conditions

Written before any launch run. Authority: owner work order 2026-10-10 §12 (launch without another routine owner
confirmation once the base-launch matrix and independent technical review pass). Values: `launch_policy.json`;
the run's exact parameters are generated by `tools/launch_manifest.py` from committed records.

## Scope

At most 250,000,000 valid training targets (3,814 updates of 65,536) of from-scratch training of the selected shape on
the final accepted corpus, newly initialized weights (seed 1), then stop. Not a full epoch; no SFT, distillation, extra
data or epochs. Hard limits: $2.40 (protected launch allocation), 1.4 h of launch-session runtime, and both
controllers' session limits.

## Schedule (prospective full run; the tranche is a prefix)

- Horizon: one exact epoch of the accepted train stream (`(stream_tokens − 1) // 2048 // 32` updates; ≈ 52.4K).
  Window permutation seeded by (seed, epoch); the tranche consumes its first 122,048 windows, each once.
- Peak LR: the selected shape's frozen protocol-18 LR. Warmup 500 updates (≈ 1 % of the horizon; the screening runs
  used 100 because their horizons were 1,396 updates), then cosine to 10 % at the horizon. At the tranche stop the
  LR is still ≈ 99 % of peak: nothing is annealed, and the scheduler state is saved so a later authorized continuation
  resumes the same schedule. Limitation: the LR was tuned at a 91M-target horizon.
- Everything else as protocol 18: sequence 2,048, micro-batch 16 × 2, AdamW 0.9/0.95, wd 0.1 on matrices only,
  clip 1.0, BF16 autocast, `torch.compile` (subject to the eager/compiled check below), flash SDPA (FA-1 explained).

## Checkpoints, preservation and lag (frozen from measurements)

- Primary: the RunPod network volume `/vol/ckpt` (staged on container disk, copied with verification, manifest-last
  `COMPLETE`). Saved at initialization, the canary boundary, every 500 updates or 15 active minutes (whichever first),
  best evaluation, SIGTERM and the tranche boundary. All tranche checkpoints are kept.
- FLOWBOX HDD replica: `tools/replicate_ckpts.py` pulls each published checkpoint (measured 12.6 MB/s ⇒ ≈ 2.5 min per
  ≈ 1.75 GB checkpoint), re-hashes every byte against the manifest, and publishes verified steps to the volume.
- **Lag limit (pause rule):** the newest valid primary checkpoint may lead the newest FLOWBOX-verified replica by at
  most 1,000 updates for at most 10 minutes; beyond that the guard stops the trainer at an optimizer boundary.
  Capacity limit: 45 GB under `/vol` (volume 50 GB; `df` cannot see the quota).
- Hugging Face (private) and Google Drive (private, owner-approved unencrypted) copies of the init, canary and final
  checkpoints with byte readback after the tranche (no pod time needed: the volume stays readable from the CPU pod).

## Canary (first 50 updates) and acceptance criteria

The tranche continues unattended only if all hold (`tools/launch_guard.py canary`):
C1 exit 0, 50 updates; C2 every loss and gradient norm finite, no skipped update, mean loss of updates 41-50 below
mean of 1-10; C3 useful targets = total targets = 50 × 65,536 (no padding); C4 steady tokens/s ≥ 90 % of the selected
shape's protocol-18 confirmation median; C5 init (step 0) and step-50 checkpoints verify byte-for-byte; C6 an
evaluation at update 50 on the registered validation split (2,583 documents, 12,348,996 bytes) with finite bpb;
C7 FLOWBOX replicas of both checkpoints verified; C8 both controllers' ledgers advancing; C9 data, tokenizer, schedule,
LR and shape identities equal to the launch manifest. The tranche then resumes from the canary checkpoint (same
schedule, same data order).

## Stop conditions during the tranche (earliest wins)

Token limit (`--stop-at 3814`); runtime deadline and USD soft/hard limits (on-pod controller and off-pod watchdog);
trainer non-finite abort; validation instability (after update 1,000, an evaluation more than 2 % above the best
earlier one); replication lag or capacity (above); stall 15 min; controller loss 3 min. Validation only; the test
split is never evaluated.

## Pre-launch qualification on the final basis

- Eager versus compiled learning equivalence, registered tolerance: two 200-update runs of the selected shape on the
  final corpus (same seed, data order and schedule; eager and `--compile`), compared by
  `tools/compile_equivalence.py`: mean |Δloss| / loss over updates 101-200 ≤ 0.5 %, and final validation bpb within
  0.3 %. If it fails, the launch uses the eager reference profile (budget permitting) or does not launch.
- Recovery on the final basis: the canary → tranche boundary is a real save/stop/resume on the final stack; the guard
  and controllers were drilled live (`controller_live_drill.md`); bitwise deterministic recovery was qualified on the
  same stack on 2026-10-10 (recovery suite) and is not repeated.

## Procedure (revised after the independent launch review of 8e510ba: REJECT, 4 blockers; all findings fixed)

1. On FLOWBOX: final shards, recount and preservation receipts committed; eager/compiled equivalence record
   committed (the manifest takes `--compile` only from a PASS); `tools/launch_manifest.py` builds the manifest with
   **absolute** session limits: soft = e0 + $2.10, hard = e0 + $2.40 (e0 = on-pod controller estimate at launch
   start; same accounting basis as the comparison: pre-pod spend $0.15 + provider uptime since the pod's start), and
   deadline = build time + 1.4 h. Manifest committed.
2. `tools/rearm_supervision.sh`: restarts the on-pod controller with those limits and switches the watchdog to a new
   session (`session_id` = run id) with the same limits.
3. `tools/authorize_launch.sh`: runs readiness v2 (`--require-stage BASE_PRETRAINING_LAUNCH`) on clean checkouts;
   only on exit 0 uploads manifest + matrix and writes `LAUNCH_AUTHORIZED`.
4. `tools/launch_tranche.sh` (self-detaching): verifies the authorization, the matrix content (valid, base stage ready,
   Train head = manifest commit), the deployed code archive hash, data, tokenizer and GPU; checks that **both
   controllers are armed to the manifest limits and their ledgers advance** (`launch_guard.py supervision`); runs the
   canary; refuses a failed canary before waiting for replicas (waits bounded to 12 min, below the 15-min stall rule);
   accepts it only for this manifest, run, host and the exact init/canary checkpoints whose FLOWBOX receipts carry
   the same manifest hashes (`CANARY_ACCEPTED` JSON, re-verified before the tranche); re-checks supervision; resumes
   **from the exact canary checkpoint path**; runs the guard in a restart loop (instability, capacity, lag with
   lead ≥ 1,000 updates for > 10 min, trainer vanished > 90 s, no update record for 10 min); requests the stop after the
   final checkpoint's replica is verified.

Effective lag bound: at most 1,000 updates ahead of the newest verified FLOWBOX replica for at most 10 minutes plus one
guard poll; at the tranche's ≈ 2.0 updates/s that is ≤ about 2,200 updates of unreplicated work in the worst case, all
of it still on the network volume (an independent store from the GPU host).

Progress definition (all three supervisors): only trainer update records (integer `step` with `loss`) or bootstrap
phase records advance progress; guard and controller logs cannot mask a hung trainer (F2).
