## Interpretation and limits

- **Tokenizer:** all three candidates lie within the pre-registered 0.5 % tie band; the tie-break (A100 training
  bytes/s) selects 24K. Per-domain bpb differs by source (32K best on most prose; 24K best on code). With a pooled
  seed SD of ≈ 0.29 % (3 dof), the bpb ordering is not resolved by these runs; the decision is the rule's outcome and
  is **provisional**. A larger vocabulary also costs more embedding parameters (24K → 32K adds ≈ 3.1M at width 384,
  ≈ 5.2M at width 640).
- **LR:** the small-model sweep chose the highest grid value (2.4e-3) and the core/deep shape checks chose the lowest
  multiplier (0.5×); the optimum may lie outside the grids. Grids were not extended after results.
- **Architecture:** the rule's outcome is **INCONCLUSIVE**: the noise band is 0.570 % (2× the pooled
  relative seed SD from the tokenizer stage); wide 20×768 clears it on equal compute (+0.917 % at T* = 1418 s,
  because it runs 9.6 % faster: 129.0K vs 117.7K tokens/s) but not on equal tokens (+0.268 %); deep 30×576 clears
  neither. Per protocol 14 §3, core 28×640 stays the **provisional** shape and seed repeats (stage 6) are not
  triggered (they run only for NEEDS_SEED_REPEATS). Caveats: one seed per shape; the LR checks put core and deep at
  the low grid edge (0.5×) and wide at its middle (1×), so the shapes may not be equally well tuned; wide also uses
  the least memory (25.0 GiB). An owner wanting to resolve core vs wide should order seed repeats and a wider LR grid
  for both, on the A100, before freezing.
- Short runs (≈ 1.2 tokens per parameter for the ~145M shapes) rank candidates under these conditions only; they do
  not establish converged quality, downstream capability or final-scale superiority. All runs are kept.
